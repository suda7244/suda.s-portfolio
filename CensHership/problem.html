The Problem

The platforms call it moderation. Many users experience it as silence.

Posts about periods, miscarriage, abortion, menopause, breastfeeding, and other women’s health topics are often hidden, blurred, rejected, or pushed out of view.

Censorship does not always look like removal. It can appear as reduced reach, softened language, or the quiet pressure to say less.

Make the invisible visible.

Evidence & Patterns

How silence is produced

Censorship is not always visible as deletion. It often appears through altered language, reduced reach, and the expectation of restriction.

✱

Words are replaced

People end up changing their language, using coded spellings, emojis, or abbreviations instead of saying things directly.

◌

Visibility is reduced

Not all censorship is obvious. Posts may remain online but become harder to search, share, or discover.

—

Silence becomes habitual

Over time, people start censoring themselves before posting, expecting restriction before it even happens.

→ These patterns are reflected in our survey data.
