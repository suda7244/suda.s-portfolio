Survey Insights

What Our Community Told Us

We collected responses from participants about their experiences with women’s health content online. These findings show how moderation systems shape visibility, language, and access to information.

26%

have experienced reduced or restricted visibility

58%

41%

Behind the survey

These responses were gathered by a team committed to making hidden experiences visible.

This project is built by students and researchers who believe women's health stories deserve visibility, care, and space to be heard.

Most commonly affected topics

Our survey suggests that women’s health content is not moderated at random. Participants most often associated censorship with topics related to sexual wellbeing, menstrual health, and breast health.

These patterns show that moderation is not only about content removal. It also affects what people choose to say, how openly they speak, and what kinds of information become harder to access online.

Silence is common

More than half of respondents suggested that people avoid using certain words or change the way they speak online.

Topics are gendered

Censorship appears concentrated around women’s bodies, reproductive health, and health education rather than neutral discussion.

Visibility is controlled

Not all moderation looks like deletion. Many users described content being quietly hidden, flagged, or pushed out of view.
